Living-world proof: Peig's scheduled movement

iOS Simulator (iPhone 17 Pro), not a physical iPhone. Real Rundale app and shared Rust engine through the C boundary, debug build of the PR branch. Inference was answered by the app's mock Endpoint transport (--ui-tests --phase3 --phase3-mock), so replies are canned, not live model output; the movement, /people, presence and save/resume are the real engine. The clock was paused with /pause and advanced with /wait. A new game starts at 07:00, so Peig is already on the lane; the app was relaunched (a real process restart) at each marked point.

What the recording shows (game time)

  1. New game, village, /pause, /debug clock reads 07:02. /people: no one here (Peig is walking from the Letter Office, her 07:00 departure).
  2. Save made mid-transit: app terminated and relaunched. Clock still 07:02, transcript restored, /people still empty.
  3. /wait 58 to 08:00. /people lists the sharp-eyed woman with the satchel (Peig). Saying "Good morning to you." gets her reply.
  4. Relaunch at 08:00, /people still lists her.
  5. /wait 61 to 09:01 (mid-walk back, her 09:00 departure). /people: no one here. Speaking draws no reply from anyone.
  6. Save made mid-walk back: relaunch. Clock 09:01, /people empty.
  7. go to the Letter Office (3 minutes on foot): she is there. /people lists her and she answers.

The same sequence runs as an automated case against the shipped world in limerick-mobile-ffi (tests::living_world::movement), twice per run on new games.
